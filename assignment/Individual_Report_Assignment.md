### Assignment objective:

The aim of this assignment is to reflect on and demonstrate the development of your entrepreneurial competencies through the lens of the societal challenge you worked on during the course, combined with insights from entrepreneurial practice, particularly guest lectures.

The assignment focuses on the three main competence areas of the **EntreComp Framework**:

- Ideas & Opportunities
- Resources
- Into Action

The assignment connects your **team-based challenge** work with your **personal development** in these competence areas. You will compare your own experiences from the Smart City challenge with insights from the entrepreneurs and practitioners featured in the **guest lectures**, as well as relevant concepts and tools introduced throughout the course, thus showing how theory and practice intersect.

The **purpose** is to reflect on what you learned from these experiences, how they relate to each other, and what they mean for your own entrepreneurial competence development (rather than simply describing what you or the guest lecturers did).

---

### Guest Lecture Reflection Notes: CAPTURE – CONNECT – REFLECT

As we asked you in the announcements, after each guest lecture you can use the **CAPTURE-CONNECT-REFLECT Cheat Sheet** to briefly record your key takeaways.

- **CAPTURE – What did I learn?**
  - Identify one specific insight, decision, experience, mistake, or piece of advice from the entrepreneur/guest that you found particularly relevant. Briefly explain why.

- **CONNECT – What does this mean for the challenge?**
  - Connect the insight to **one specific situation, decision, or challenge** in your team's Smart City project. How does the entrepreneur/guest's experience support, challenge, or change your team's approach in your personal opinion?

- **REFLECT – What does this tell me about myself?**
  - Compare the entrepreneur/guest's experience with **your own behavior, thinking, skills, or contribution**. What does this reveal about one of your strengths, weaknesses, assumptions, or entrepreneurial competencies?

We recommend completing these short reflection notes after each guest lecture and **include them in the Appendix of your final individual report**.

The notes serve as an **evidence bank and preparation** for your final reflection. In Parts 1–4 below, select and integrate the guest-lecture insights that are most relevant to your challenge experience and personal development.

**Do not simply copy your mini-reflections into the main report.** The final reflection should go a step further by selecting, comparing, connecting, and synthesizing insights across your course experiences.

The Guest Lecture Reflection Notes in the Appendix are **not assessed separately for writing quality**. The assessment focuses on how effectively you use and reflect on relevant insights in the main report.

### Alternative Sources for the Connection to Practice

If you missed a guest lecture or would like to complement the guest lectures with another perspective, you may use a relevant external source featuring an entrepreneur, innovator, or entrepreneurial practitioner as an alternative for the **Connection to Practice**.

Suitable sources may include, for example:

- Podcasts
- Videos or recorded interviews
- An article featuring or written by an entrepreneur or innovator
- Another credible sources that provide insights into the experiences, decisions, challenges, or practices of entrepreneurs or innovators

The source should be **relevant to the topic you are reflecting on** and should provide a sufficiently substantive basis for reflection. General websites or sources that merely define entrepreneurial concepts are not suitable substitutes for an entrepreneur's or innovator's experience.

When using an alternative source, you must **clearly identify and reference the source** so that it can be traced by the reader.

You should use alternative sources in the same way as guest-lecture insights:

**specific insight → connection to your challenge experience → reflection on your own entrepreneurial competencies → learning and/or future action**

Alternative sources can therefore be used to meet the **Connection to Practice** requirements in Parts 1–4. However, the course guest lectures remain the primary source for connecting your course experience to entrepreneurial practice. The highest score for the criteria **Integration of Guest Lectures & Entrepreneurial Practice** requires meaningful engagement with multiple course guest lectures. External sources can complement or substitute for missed guest lectures but cannot fully replace them.

### Assignment brief:

#### Part 1. Challenge Reflection (Ideas & Opportunities) (≈500 words, visuals allowed)

- Select one **opportunity** your team explored during the Smart City challenge.
- In your own words, explain:
  - What unmet need or problem was identified?
  - Why you personally found it meaningful or relevant?
  - Which ideation tools you used and how they shape the opportunity?
- **Connection to practice:** Refer to at least three insights from three different guest lectures that inspired how to further develop your competences in Ideation and Opportunity Discovery. Explain how this could strengthen, challenge, or change the approach used by your team in the challenge. If you missed guest lectures or want to complement insights, you may use relevant alternative entrepreneurial-practice sources as described above.

---

### Part 2. Resource Reflection (Resources) (≈400 words, table or matrix allowed)

- Analyze the **resources** needed to move this opportunity forward, considering:
  - Human capital (skills/expertise).
  - Social capital (partners, networks, stakeholders).
  - Physical/financial resources (prototyping, funding, technology, infrastructure, etc.).
- Compare these requirements with the resources your team currently has. Reflect on:
  - Which important resources are already available?
  - Which resources are missing?
  - Where do you personally see yourself contributing most strongly?

- **Connection to Practice:**
  - Reflect on how at least **one guest lecturer** described mobilizing resources, for example through leveraging networks, bootstrapping, partnerships, funding, or other approaches.
  - How does the guest lecturer's experience relate to your own resource analysis? What can you learn from it for developing your own competencies in mobilizing resources?
  - If you missed guest lectures or want to complement insights, you may use relevant alternative entrepreneurial-practice sources as described above.

---

### Part 3. Action Reflection (Into Action) (≈400 words, link to examples if available)

- Reflect on your **actions and behaviors** during the course:
  - How did you deal with **uncertainty** in the ideation process?
  - Which choices, actions, or behaviors had the most impact on your team's progress?
  - How did you **communicate/pitch** your ideas to others,
  - What feedback did you receive and how did you respond to it?

- **Connection to practice:**
  - Compare your own experience with how guest lecturers described entrepreneurial action, for example when **dealing with uncertainty, making decisions, experimenting, communicating ideas, or pitching**.
  - Refer to at least **two specific insights** from **two different guest lectures**.
  - What similarities or differences do you recognize compared with your own behavior? What do these comparisons tell you about your own strengths and areas for development?
  - If you missed guest lectures or want to complement insights, you may use relevant alternative entrepreneurial-practice sources as described above.

---

### Part 4. Personal Development Plan (Self-Reflection) (≈400 words, bullet-point format allowed)

- Based on the 7 course learning objectives, identify **two entrepreneurial competences** you want to strengthen further.
- For each competence:
  - Explain why you selected it.
  - Reflect on what your course experience revealed about your current strengths and/or areas for improvement.
  - Formulate a SMART learning goal.
  - Suggest at least one concrete activity you will undertake after the course to further develop this competence (e.g., join a startup team, take a design thinking MOOC, participate in a student board, or work on your own entrepreneurial project).

- **Connection to practice:**
  - Which guest lecturer(s) do you see as a role model or source of inspiration for developing these competencies, and why?
    - You may draw on one guest lecturer or combine insights from several guest lecturers.
  - If you missed guest lectures or want to complement them, you may also draw on an entrepreneur or innovator from an alternative source as described above.

### Evaluation criteria:

**Ideas and Opportunities (20 %):**

- **Excellent — 20 points**
  - Clearly and insightfully explains the selected opportunity, the underlying unmet need/problem, and its personal relevance. Demonstrates a strong understanding of how and why specific ideation tools shaped the opportunity, rather than merely listing the tools used. Critically reflects on the team's approach, important choices or assumptions, and what the student learned about identifying and developing entrepreneurial opportunities.

- **Good — 16 points**
  - Clearly explains the opportunity, its personal relevance, and how ideation tools influenced its development. Demonstrates meaningful reflection on the team's approach and the student's own learning, although some connections, assumptions, or implications could be explored more deeply.

- **Satisfactory — 12 points**
  - Adequately explains the opportunity and ideation process and provides some personal reflection. However, the discussion remains partly descriptive, with limited analysis of how the tools shaped the opportunity or what the student learned about opportunity identification and development.

- **Weak — 8 points**
  - Primarily describes the opportunity and activities undertaken. Ideation tools are mostly listed or described rather than meaningfully analyzed. Personal reflection and evidence of competence development are limited.

- **Insufficient — 4 points**
  - Major required elements are missing or there is little evidence of understanding and reflection regarding Ideas & Opportunities.

- **Missing — 0 points**

  - Meaningfully integrates insights from 3 or more different guest lectures across the report. Guest-lecture insights are specific and clearly connected to the challenge and the student's own experiences or competencies.

Some reflection is demonstrated, but connections tend to remain relatively straightforward or isolated, with limited comparison or synthesis across experiences.
